# How to read this

AI Safety Assessments grades the evidence behind developments in AI safety and
makes judgments about what happens next that can be checked when their deadlines
pass. Each issue states how well each development is supported, which sources it
rests on, and what would change the assessment. The scheme below is the same in
every issue.

## Reading an issue

Each issue opens with the **key judgments**: a bottom line, then each judgment
with the findings it rests on. Directly under them is the **source summary**: what
that week's evidence base was strong and weak at, where the sourcing concentrated,
and what could not be reached. Read it before deciding how much weight a judgment
bears.

The rest of the issue starts collapsed. Open the sections you need.

- **2 Changes from prior assessment.** Earlier judgments that resolved, were
  re-rated or met new evidence this week. Judgments with no significant
  development are not listed.
- **3 The week in detail.** Developments by area, each tagged with its taxonomy
  area and linked to its primary source.
- **4 Watchlist.** Signals that would move a judgment without resolving it.
- **5 Evidence table.** Every source the issue relies on, with what that source
  itself says (the *Claim actually made* column), its rating, its origin (the
  underlying record it traces back to) and the rows that corroborate it.

A bracketed number such as [4] links to that row of the evidence table; hovering
it shows the source and rating. Row, origin and judgment numbers restart each
issue. A judgment from an earlier issue is written *Issue-7 KJ-1* (key judgment
1 of Issue 7).

## Coverage

Every issue's collection checks every area of the taxonomy. **Technical safety:**
alignment · AI control · interpretability and model transparency · science of
evaluations · strategic awareness · red teaming · safety cases. **Security:** AI
security · misuse and incidents. **Societal impacts:** CBRN×AI · human influence ·
societal resilience · model welfare and moral status · agent economics.
**Strategy and governance:** policy and regulation by jurisdiction · developer
governance · geopolitics and diffusion · field-building · AI in defence.

Only areas with a development appear in an issue. An area's absence means
nothing was located that week, not that nothing happened.

## Findings and judgments

A **finding** is something that has already happened, graded by how well it is
corroborated. A **judgment** is a forecast about something not yet observed, with
a probability band, a deadline and a named audience. Probability language is not
used on findings, and corroboration statuses are not used on forecasts: putting a
probability word on a settled fact overstates how much is uncertain.

Each judgment names who it is for: **decision-makers** (funders, organisation
leads, policymakers), **contributors** (people choosing where to work in the
field) or the **public**.

## Probability bands

Judgments use the PHIA Probability Yardstick.

| Band | Range |
|---|---|
| remote chance | >0–≈5% |
| highly unlikely | ≈10–20% |
| unlikely | ≈25–35% |
| realistic possibility | ≈40–<50% |
| likely / probable | ≈55–75% |
| highly likely | ≈80–90% |
| almost certain | ≈95–<100% |

A judgment that falls between two bands is placed in the one that reflects the
assessment. A claim that can only be stated as *almost certain* or *remote chance*
usually carries little information, and is re-scoped or recorded as a finding
instead.

Each judgment also carries **confidence** (high, moderate or low) in a separate
clause, with its basis stated. The band is a statement about the world.
Confidence is a statement about the evidence: how much the band would move if
more evidence arrived. The two are not combined.

- **High:** two or more independent origins, no credible contradiction, and at
  least one source speaking against its own interest or with a track record.
- **Moderate:** no credible contradiction, but the key evidence comes from one
  origin, or one assumption the judgment depends on is unsupported.
- **Low:** one origin with an interest in the claim, or an assumption that cannot
  be tested, or credible contradicting evidence.

## Axes

Each judgment's header names the question its resolution bears on, fixed when
the judgment is first published: **capability pace** (how fast frontier capability is
advancing) · **misalignment incidence** (how often misaligned behaviour is
observed) · **governance response** (how far and how fast binding governance
moves) · **developer conduct** (what frontier model developers choose to do) ·
**geopolitics and diffusion** (how capability spreads across states and actors) ·
**other**. The axis allows resolved judgments to be grouped by question rather
than by the week they were made.

## Finding statuses

Findings carry a corroboration status, taken from the Admiralty information axis:

- **established:** confirmed across independent origins
- **single-origin credible:** from one solid source, not independently confirmed
- **contested:** credible evidence points both ways
- **unsubstantiated:** asserted, not yet supported

## Evidence ratings

Every row of the evidence table carries an adapted Admiralty code such as **B2**.
The letter grades the source; the digit grades how far the specific claim is
confirmed by others. The two are independent: A3 is a reliable source making a
claim nobody else has confirmed, and C1 is a self-interested announcement that
independent parties have confirmed.

**Source (A–F):** **A** strong track record, or a primary source speaking against
its own interest · **B** primary source, neutral incentive, methodology visible ·
**C** primary but self-flattering, or reputable secondary with named sourcing ·
**D** weakly sourced, or interested party without methodology · **E** known
unreliable channel · **F** cannot be judged.

**Information (1–6):** **1** confirmed by independent origins · **2** consistent
with other reporting, not independently confirmed · **3** plausible,
uncorroborated · **4** doubtful · **5** contradicted by other evidence ·
**6** cannot be judged.

Corroboration counts only across distinct underlying origins. Three articles
quoting one report count once, and two observers of the same document are one
origin. The codes are labels: they are not added, weighted or otherwise combined.

In an issue, hovering over or focusing any rating, band, status, confidence level
or axis shows its meaning.

## Limits

**Coverage.** Collection covers the indexed web only: no insider sources,
embargoed material or private communication.

**Track record.** Every judgment is recorded with its wording, band, confidence
and deadline fixed at publication, and is scored when its deadline passes or it
resolves. Until enough judgments have resolved, the method can be inspected but
its accuracy has not been measured.

## What is published

The issues are published here. The analyst notes are not: worksheets, rating
and verification records, and drafting notes.

The notes are withheld so they are not taken for part of the assessment. Each
evidence row publishes the source with a link, what the source says, and the
rating with its basis, so each claim can be checked against its source without
them.

## Visit counts

Visits are counted with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and stores nothing in
your browser. It records which pages are viewed, the referring site, and the
country, browser and screen size of each visit, in aggregate. On issue pages it
also counts which sections are opened, whether definitions and evidence
references are used, and which source sites readers follow. The counts are used
only to see which parts of the publication are read.
